This report records the read-only V2.2 audit. It does not authorize a PCB,

## Proven inherited facts

- J1 is the only active SNAC-style bus on the V2.1-derived board.
- J2 uses the same Molex 48393-0003 nine-contact connector and exact registered
  3D model, but contacts 1–9 and all shell pads are electrically NC. It remains
  reserved for a future characterized interface.
- J3 is the real USB-C programming/power connector. J1/J2 are explicitly
  non-USB connector applications.
- The 56.295 x 62.000 mm, four-layer, 1.20 mm board has no unrouted items in the
  inherited baseline. DRC has 0 errors and 0 unconnected items, with only the
  three accepted historical `track_dangling` warnings; ERC and schematic/PCB
  parity are both zero.
- J1 is at `(4.525, 50.525) mm`, rotation `0°`; J2 is at
  `(22.525, 50.525) mm`, rotation `0°`. Their mouths face outward through the
  lower edge.
- The lower-right edge is occupied by TP2 GND `(43.500, 54.000)`, TP3 TX
  `(46.700, 54.000)`, and TP4 RX `(49.900, 54.000)`. These are 2.0 mm plated
  pads with 1.0 mm finished holes on 3.2 mm pitch.
- The repository does not define whether J4 must work simultaneously with J1
  or only as a selected alternate. That choice changes the electrical
  architecture and is the sole blocking product decision identified by this
  audit.

## Architecture comparison

### A. Independent protected J4 interface

J4 receives five unique signal nets and its own protection/drive circuitry.
`CMD`, `CLK`, and `ATT` repeat the J1 input pattern: series resistance,
defined pulldown and rail clamp. `DATA` and `ACK` repeat the J1 open-drain MOSFET
pattern. J1 and J4 never share active signal nets.

Candidate unused ESP32 module connections, subject to firmware confirmation:

| J4 role | Candidate module pin | GPIO | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CMD input | 10 | GPIO25 | Input |
| CLK input | 11 | GPIO26 | Input |
| ATT input | 12 | GPIO27 | Input |
| DATA gate | 27 | GPIO16 | Output to open-drain stage |
| ACK gate | 28 | GPIO17 | Output to open-drain stage |

These module pads are unassigned in the current netlist and avoid the existing
BOOT, UART, STATUS and J1 assignments. Firmware may nevertheless have implicit
uses, so they are a proposal, not a released allocation.

Power must also be independent. J4 pin 8 must not be tied directly to
`PS_8V_RAW`. Two safe variants exist:

1. Leave J4 pin 8 electrically NC and require the board to be powered from J1
   or J3. This is minimal and prevents power backfeed, but J4 cannot power the
   board by itself.
2. Give J4 its own PTC and reverse-current blocker, and add equivalent reverse
   blocking to the J1 branch before OR-ing both sources into the existing buck.
   This supports either cable as a power source without cross-feeding consoles,
   but it modifies the validated J1 power path and requires component/rating and
   transient validation.

Advantages: true simultaneous operation, no signal contention, straightforward
firmware ownership, and a failure on one bus need not corrupt the other.
Costs: five GPIOs, a duplicate protection/drive channel, extra placement area,
and a deliberate power-input design.

If simultaneous operation is required, this is the recommended architecture.

### B. Selected/muxed or isolated shared interface

J1 and J4 feed a hardware selection/isolation stage, and only one connector is
electrically attached to the existing J1 protection and ESP32 interface at a
time. The selector must cover all five signals, preserve the open-drain behavior
of DATA/ACK, tolerate the console-side voltage range, use break-before-make
switching, and default to a defined high-impedance or known-safe port during
reset and power transitions. Protection is required on each external branch
before vulnerable mux pins. Pin-8 power still needs separate reverse blocking
or must remain NC on J4; selecting signals alone does not prevent power
backfeed.

Advantages: fewer ESP32 GPIOs and reuse of the established firmware bus.
Costs: more analog-switch qualification, a new selection state/failure mode,
added series impedance/capacitance, and no simultaneous J1/J4 operation.

If J4 is only an alternate physical cable port, this is the recommended
minimal-function architecture, provided the selected switch is fully qualified
for the real PS1 levels and timing.

### C. Direct parallel

Directly joining J4 to the J1 signal and power nets is unsafe and is rejected.
CMD, CLK and ATT are externally driven inputs that may come from different
console ports or consoles; parallel sources can contend. DATA and ACK are
active open-drain outputs whose timing and pull conditions would be shared
across both cables. Directly joining pin 8 creates a console-to-console power
backfeed path. A user instruction to insert only one cable is not a hardware
interlock and cannot protect against accidental dual insertion, hot-plugging,
startup states or a normal USB cable inserted into a non-USB-shaped application.

A proven single-cable constraint would still require enforced isolation,
reverse-current protection and a fail-safe selection mechanism. Once those are
added, the design is architecture B rather than a defensible direct parallel.

## Future implementation and reroute scope

No routing is authorized by this report. A later implementation should limit
changes to:

- J4 and its new signal/protection/power components and nets;
- the five selected unused U1 pads for architecture A, or the selector-control
  GPIO(s) for architecture B;
- local J4 GND/power connections;
- the terminal branches of GND, UART_TX and UART_RX needed to move TP2–TP4;
- local silkscreen around J4 and the relocated SERVICE/DEBUG row.

J1/J2/J3 placement, USB-A model registration, USB-C, ESP32/antenna geometry,
RESET/BOOT, STATUS/PWR, outline, and unrelated placement/copper remain outside
scope. The future build should preserve compatible copper and only unroute the
explicit affected nets if required. Full rerouting would need separate review
because it would discard validated V2.1 copper.

Primary risks are: unknown simultaneous-versus-selected product behavior,
power backfeed, switch voltage/timing compatibility, firmware GPIO conflicts,
space for duplicated protection, cable-overmold/enclosure fit, and disturbing
the validated UART or power routing.

## Blocking product decision

Choose exactly one requirement before PCB work begins:

> Must J1 and J4 operate simultaneously as two independent active SNAC buses,
> or is J4 only an alternate selected physical port with at most one active bus?

Select **independent** for simultaneous operation (architecture A). Select
**alternate** for one-at-a-time operation (architecture B). Architecture C is
not an acceptable release option.

## Possible major combined release: PC-wireless PS1 game loader

A teammate is developing a PC-wireless PS1 game loader. This may become a major
combined release, but it is not part of the current V2.2 PCB work and must not
be merged by assumption.

- The PC is the game-image source.
- Do not assume an SD slot. Local SD storage is optional only after buffering,
  offline behavior and storage requirements are explicitly defined.
- Game-stream traffic and the PS1 game-data interface must remain electrically
  separate from controller/SNAC buses. They may share system-level power or a
  processor only after isolation, bandwidth, reset-domain and fault analysis;
  they must not reuse or parallel J1/J4 console-controller signal nets.
- Do not begin a schematic, PCB, folder/project rename, or combined-board merge
  until the teammate supplies:
  - schematic;
  - PCB and/or source netlist;
  - firmware;
  - exact PS1 game-data electrical and connector interface;
  - wireless protocol and radio implementation;
  - buffering and optional-storage requirements;
  - sustained/burst throughput and latency targets;
  - supported PS1 hardware revisions;
  - complete power rails, current peaks, sequencing and source requirements.

After those inputs exist, perform a separate partitioning review covering RF
coexistence, memory/buffer sizing, PS1 bus timing, galvanic/logic-domain
boundaries, power budget and failure containment before choosing whether a
combined PCB is appropriate.
