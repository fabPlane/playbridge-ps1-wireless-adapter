## Corrected intended architecture

J4 is **not** another controller/SNAC bus and is **not** an alternate copy of
J1. The intended future data path is:

`PC/browser -> Wi-Fi -> PlayBridge ESP32 -> UART GPIO17/TX and GPIO16/RX -> J4 -> donor-cable harness -> PS1 serial port`

J4 uses a USB-A connector only as a readily available nine-contact physical
interconnect. It is explicitly **NOT USB**. A normal USB host, charger or device
must never be connected to J4.

The provisional J4 electrical roles are only:

- PlayBridge UART TX on ESP32 GPIO17;
- PlayBridge UART RX on ESP32 GPIO16;
- GND.

Exact J4 contact numbers are intentionally unassigned until the PS1 serial
harness pinout and electrical requirements are received. All other J4 contacts,
including any contact that resembles USB VBUS and all shell pads, remain
reserved/NC. No J4 power rail, console-power input, USB data pair, controller
signal or board 3V3 connection is assumed.

## Preserved connector identities

- J1 remains the existing active PS1 controller/SNAC donor-harness bus with its
  current pinout and protection. Nothing in the J4 serial proposal changes or
  parallels J1.
- J2 remains electrically all-NC/reserved. Contacts 1–9 and the shell pads have
  no assigned nets.
- J3 remains the real USB-C power/program/debug connector and its orientation,
  USB-UART circuitry and power path remain unchanged.
- J4 serial traffic and the J1 controller/SNAC bus must remain electrically
  separate.

### Drive-contention rule

An external USB-UART adapter and a PS1 serial harness must never drive the same
UART line simultaneously. Series resistance can limit fault current but does
not turn two push-pull transmitters into a safe shared bus.

The existing TP4 receive net can also be driven by the onboard CH340C path.
Attaching an external adapter to TP4 while the onboard USB-UART is powered or
active can cause contention. Future documentation and silkscreen must specify
the allowed connection/power state, or hardware isolation must enforce it.

If the future J4 design keeps GPIO16/GPIO17 separate from UART0, the present
TP2/TP3/TP4 row does not electrically share the PS1 serial link. If later
requirements move debug access onto GPIO16/GPIO17, the following isolation
options must be evaluated before routing.

## UART isolation options to evaluate

### 1. Series isolation resistors

Place series resistors near the ESP32 and/or external connector. They reduce
edge ringing and limit current during accidental contention. They are compact
and preserve a direct UART path.

They do not prevent two transmitters from driving opposite logic levels, do
not guarantee powered-off isolation and do not solve incompatible voltage
levels. This option is acceptable only after directionality, voltage levels and
the permitted connection states are proven.

### 2. UART mux or analog/digital switch

A mux/switch can enforce one selected external source, provide a safe default
state and isolate an unpowered endpoint. The device must be qualified for the
actual PS1 serial voltage range, directionality, bandwidth, power-off behavior,
leakage and fail-safe state. Selection behavior during reset and firmware
startup must be defined.

This is the appropriate class of solution if GPIO16/GPIO17 must be shared
between the PS1 harness and a debug adapter, but no device or topology is
selected in this report.

### 3. Separate debug UART

Retain the existing UART0/CH340/TP2–TP4 path for programming and service, while
dedicating GPIO17/TX and GPIO16/RX to J4. This avoids normal signal sharing and
minimizes contention between the debug and PS1 serial domains. Firmware UART
availability, boot logging, pin ownership and PS1 protocol timing must still be
confirmed.

No option is chosen or routed here. The teammate firmware and PS1 electrical
requirements are prerequisites.

## V3.0 combined-subsystem boundary

The PC-wireless PS1 game-loader work is being evaluated in this **V3.0 major
development line**. Renaming the project does not prove the teammate subsystem,
authorize a board merge, or authorize manufacturing.

The PC/browser is the game-image source. Do not assume:

- an SD slot or mandatory local storage;
- a CD-drive bus connection;
- a parallel expansion interface;
- reuse of J1/J4 controller or serial nets for game-stream traffic.

Any SD or other local storage remains optional until buffering, offline and
capacity requirements are known. Game-stream traffic and the PS1 game-data
interface must remain electrically separate from controller/SNAC buses and
from the J4 PS1 serial service path unless a later, documented interface review
proves otherwise.

Do not begin the V3.0 schematic/PCB merge until the teammate provides:

1. Firmware and protocol implementation.
2. Exact PS1 serial electrical levels and voltage tolerances.
3. Signal directionality, including startup, idle and power-off behavior.
4. Baud rate, sustained/burst throughput and latency targets.
5. Exact PS1 serial donor-harness connector/contact pinout.
6. Supported PS1 hardware revisions and any revision-dependent behavior.
7. Power-source behavior, rail requirements, current peaks, sequencing and
   backfeed constraints.

The broader combined-release review will additionally need the teammate's
schematic, PCB/netlist, wireless protocol, buffering/storage requirements and
fault-containment plan before deciding whether a combined PCB is appropriate.
