## J2 versus J4 decision

J2 is physically present at `(22.525, 50.525) mm`, faces outward through the lower edge, uses the same approved keyed nine-contact Molex 48393-0003 connector family as J1, and has no nets on contacts 1-9 or its shell pads. Its value and documentation identify it as the reserved rear-serial **NOT-USB** interface. Those facts make J2 the preferred candidate for the requested future path:

`PC/browser -> Wi-Fi -> ESP32 -> GPIO17/TX + GPIO16/RX + GND -> keyed NOT-USB harness -> PS1 serial port`

No known evidence presently requires a distinct J4:

- electrically, J2 is completely free;
- mechanically, it is already edge-mounted in the intended connector family and orientation;
- no enclosure, cable-overmold, strain-relief, or mating-access conflict has been supplied;
- no simultaneous second use of J2 has been defined;
- its future reference/value/silkscreen can identify the serial role and **NOT USB** warning without creating a third connector.

Therefore: **do not add J4 at this gate**. J2 is recommended in principle, but no J2 contact assignment or copper change is approved until the missing harness and electrical inputs are supplied. Because J4 is not justified, this report intentionally provides no candidate J4 placement. A distinct J4 would become defensible only if evidence proves J2 must remain available simultaneously for a different interface, cannot mate or clear the enclosure/harness, or cannot be labeled safely. The previously discussed lower-right third-shell concept would overlap TP2-TP4 and require their relocation, adding mechanical and routing risk.

## UART and contention findings

- TP2 is GND.
- TP3 is `UART_TX`: ESP32 module pin 35, GPIO1/U0TXD, shared with CH340C pin 3 (its receive input).
- TP4 is `UART_RX`: ESP32 module pin 34, GPIO3/U0RXD, shared with CH340C pin 2 through the existing R16 1 kOhm / R17 2 kOhm divider.
- TP2/TP3/TP4 therefore expose UART0 on GPIO1/GPIO3; they do **not** expose GPIO16/GPIO17.
- ESP32 module pin 27/GPIO16 and pin 28/GPIO17 have no assigned nets in the inherited netlist. Their firmware ownership and boot-time behavior are still unknown.

If the PS1 serial link remains on GPIO17/GPIO16, it is electrically separate from the onboard CH340C and TP3/TP4 UART0 path. Retaining this separate debug UART is the evidence-supported recommendation and avoids normal PS1/debug contention.

An external adapter correctly driving TP4 can still contend with the onboard CH340C transmitter whenever that device is powered or active; an adapter incorrectly driving TP3 can contend with ESP32 UART0 TX. Do not connect adapter power, and do not permit simultaneous transmitters, until allowed power/connection states are documented. Series resistors can limit fault current and ringing but cannot arbitrate push-pull drivers or guarantee powered-off isolation. A mux/switch is warranted only if one ESP32 UART must be shared and must then be selected for verified voltage range, bandwidth, leakage, fail-safe state, reset behavior, and power-off isolation. Current evidence favors neither added series isolation nor a mux on GPIO16/GPIO17 because the separate UART0 debug path already exists.

## Missing prerequisites

Do not assign contacts or begin implementation until the following are authoritative:

1. Teammate schematic/netlist and interface ownership.
2. Firmware and PS1 serial protocol implementation.
3. Exact J2/J4 donor-harness contact pinout, connector views, keying, and cable wiring.
4. PS1 serial logic levels and tolerances at every endpoint.
5. TX/RX direction, idle state, reset/startup behavior, and powered-off behavior.
6. Baud rate, sustained/burst throughput, latency, and buffering requirements.
7. Supported PS1 revisions and revision-dependent behavior.
8. Power-source/rail requirements, current, sequencing, grounding, and backfeed constraints.
9. Enclosure opening, connector access, cable-overmold, bend radius, and strain-relief dimensions.
